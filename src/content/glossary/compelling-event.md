---
term: "Compelling Event"
short: "A Compelling Event is a dated reason, with real consequences, why the customer must act now, built from implicated pain and agreed Metrics rather than the seller's quarter end."
group: qualification
related: [implicate-the-pain, metrics, champion]
maxims: [11]
order: 13
---

A Compelling Event is something with a date and a cost on the customer's side of the table. A contract expiring. A regulation landing in April. A product launch that fails without the capability you sell. A new CFO who has told the team the number must move by Q3. If the date passes and nothing bad happens to them, it was not compelling. It was a preference.

The question it answers is the one that quietly kills more deals than any competitor: why now?

The mistake is on both sides of the desk. Sellers think a discount that expires on Friday is a Compelling Event. It is not, and customers know it, which is why they call you on the following Tuesday to ask if it is still available. The other mistake is adding a C to the acronym. Some people run MEDDPICCC with a third C for Compelling Event. My view is that this tells the world their understanding of MEDDPICC is shallow. The urgency is already in there. Implicated Pain plus agreed Metrics plus a Champion who owns the outcome gives you a customer who needs to move. You do not need a ninth letter. You need to do the other eight properly.

How I use it. Urgency is an emotion, and you cannot email an emotion. So I do not try to manufacture a Compelling Event with pressure. I look for the one that already exists in the customer's world, and if there is none, I help the Champion build one out of the consequences of doing nothing, with a date on it. If there is no date and no consequence, "later" wins every time, and I would rather plan for that than forecast against it.
