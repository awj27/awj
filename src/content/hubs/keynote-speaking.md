The first stage I stood on was at Potters, a holiday resort, in 2001. Entertainer and DJ, three years before I sold anything. You learn fast in that job what a room feels like when it is with you, and the exact moment it leaves. Twenty years of selling later, that is still the only metric I care about on a stage.

Most sales kickoff keynotes lose the room by the third slide. A speaker who has never carried a number tells a team that has to carry one how to feel about it. The team claps, goes to the bar, and sells on Monday exactly the way they sold on Friday.

A keynote has one job. Monday has to be different.

## What I speak about

Four things, and they overlap.

Qualification. MEDDPICC, from the person who wrote the book on it and runs the company that teaches it. What the letters mean, where teams fake them, and what the framework looks like when it is a way of thinking rather than a form in the CRM.

Deal inspection. How leaders run deal reviews and forecast calls that find the truth. Why "I'm working it hard" is a confession. Why the B in B-Player stands for busy, and why there is no such thing as a C-Player.

Sales as a thinking discipline. The habits of A-Players, who are uncomfortable being comfortable, and the patterns that separate them from theatre.

The human side of selling. Imposter syndrome, which only the self-aware suffer from. Authenticity, which is felt, not found. Urgency, which is an emotion and cannot be emailed. The things reps carry into the room that no methodology covers.

I speak at sales kickoffs, revenue summits and leadership offsites, in person around the world and virtually.

## What a good SKO keynote does

It gives the room one way of thinking they can use on Monday. One. If your team leaves with a framework for their next deal review, you have had a good keynote. If they leave with twelve takeaways and a quote for the wall, they have had a nice morning.

It uses your deals. The best session I can run is built around your team's actual opportunities, your stage gates and the objections your reps hear every week. When the examples are theirs, nobody can dismiss it as theory.

It respects the leaders in the room. Your managers hear their own language in the talk and can carry it into Tuesday's pipeline review without translating.

And it entertains, because people do not act on what bored them.

Now what it does not do. It does not pitch my company. If you want training, that lives at meddicc.com, and the keynote stands on its own.

It does not tell your team they are amazing for an hour, because motivational soundbites fade by Tuesday and your reps know it before you do. It does not recycle last year's deck with your logo on the title slide. And it does not pretend selling is easy. The room is full of people who know it is not.

## Three formats

Keynote. A 30 to 60 minute talk built around the MEDDICC framework: the principles, the failure modes, and the patterns that separate A-Players from theatre. For conferences and revenue summits.

Sales kickoff. A bespoke session designed around your deals, your stage gates and your objection patterns. It goes deeper than a keynote because the material is yours.

Master of ceremonies. I host the event end to end. Set the tone, hold the energy, conduct the panels, deliver the close. Best for SKOs and revenue summits, where the day has to feel like one event rather than eight.

## How I prepare

I treat every keynote like it has to be the greatest of all time. At MEDDICC we call it the KEYGOAT mindset. Nobody is claiming to be the greatest speaker alive. The standard is for the talk: painstakingly curated, built from scratch for the room, and engineered to send every person out ready to take immediate action.

That starts with your deals. Before I write a word, I want the opportunities your team is working, the stage gates they have to pass, and the objections they keep hearing. I want to know what your leaders inspect and what they let slide. The talk is written from that, which is why no two are the same.

Then it gets rehearsed until the room gets a performance rather than a presentation. The Potters training never left.
