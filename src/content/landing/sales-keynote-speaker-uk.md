A sales keynote has one job: change what the room does on Monday. Most of them fail it. The speaker is funny, the slides are slick, the team claps, and by Tuesday nobody can tell you what they are doing differently. The energy was real. It had nothing to attach to.

I keynote sales kickoffs, conferences and revenue summits, in person worldwide and virtually, for organisations including CrowdStrike, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services and Dun and Bradstreet. Before that I spent twenty years selling and leading sales teams, at Oracle, Sprinklr, Tealium, Poq and Branch among others, and I wrote MEDDICC, the book that has sold more than 100,000 copies. I am based in the UK. The passport gets used.

## What I talk about

Enterprise sales, MEDDICC and MEDDPICC, sales as a thinking discipline, and the patterns that separate A-players from theatre.

The keynote runs 30 to 60 minutes and is built around MEDDICC: the principles, the failure modes, and what the best sellers do that the rest miss. Why the deal that was going great died at procurement. Why the Champion who loved the demo never sold a thing internally. Why the Economic Buyer nobody met said no to a proposal nobody wrote for them. A room full of sellers recognises every one of those, because every one of them has lived it.

I do not do motivation without a method. Fired up is the aim, and the KEYGOAT mindset is the standard: every keynote treated as though it has to be the greatest of all time. But fired up with nothing to run is gone by the first objection. The room leaves with frameworks they can apply on Monday, not soundbites that fade by Tuesday.

## Three formats

A keynote, for a conference or a revenue summit. 30 to 60 minutes, cut for the room in front of it.

A sales kickoff session, built around your team's actual deals, stage gates and objection patterns. This goes deeper than a keynote can, because the examples are yours, and the team leaves with something specific to inspect on the next forecast call.

Master of ceremonies, for the whole event. Set the tone, hold the energy, run the panels, deliver the close. Best for SKOs and revenue summits where you want one voice holding the day together.

All three work in person anywhere in the world. The keynote and the kickoff session also work virtually, for distributed teams, leadership offsites and summits.

## How we build it

The best sessions start with the organiser, which is why I would rather have an hour of your time before the event than a polished brief. Tell me the deals your team is stuck on. Tell me which stage gates they skip and which objections they hear most. Tell me the one thing you need them to do differently afterwards. The talk gets built around those answers, so what is on stage is your pipeline, not a case study from somebody else's.

Hannah Klug, VP Sales Enablement at Arctic Wolf Networks: "OMG Andy owned the stage!" Mandira Mehra, CMO of MRI Software: "Andy helped take our GKO to the next level." And Susan Kinser at Net Health, who was grateful I flew halfway across the world to ignite something big within her organisation. I did. It was worth the flight.
