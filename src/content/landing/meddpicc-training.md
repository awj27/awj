Most MEDDPICC training fails in the same place. The sessions go well, the slides are good, everyone leaves with a scorecard, and by the third forecast call the team is back to reporting deals instead of inspecting them. The training taught the letters. It never changed the behaviour.

I have sat on every side of this. I spent twenty years selling and leading sales teams, and early on I was playing sales on hard mode while everyone around me seemed to be on easy. Discovering MEDDIC was the turning point. It took me from the B-player version of myself to the A-player version. I wrote the book about it, MEDDICC, which has sold more than 100,000 copies, and I founded the company that built the MEDDICC Masterclass, which has more than 35,000 graduates. The course lives at meddicc.com. This page is my opinion on what good training looks like, written so you can judge any provider against it, mine included.

## Who needs it

Anyone selling a deal with more than one decision maker and a procurement process at the end. Account executives running complex deals. The managers who run their forecast calls, because a manager who cannot inspect a deal cannot coach one. Founders selling their own product, who usually need it most and get it last.

The test is simple. Has a rep ever told you a deal is going great? Did you have any way of checking? If not, that is the gap the training fills.

## What it should be, and what it should not

Good training does one thing: it changes what a seller does in a live deal.

After it, Metrics are quantified rather than gestured at. The Economic Buyer is named, and met. The Champion is tested: does this person sell for you when you are not in the room, or do they just like you? The Paper Process is mapped before the end of the quarter rather than discovered during it. Competition is named, including the one that wins most often, doing nothing.

Bad training is a checklist with a certificate stapled to it. Eight boxes, ticked. It feels like qualification. It produces the same fiction you had before, better organised, and it hands everyone a shared vocabulary for being wrong. MEDDPICC is a way of thinking about a deal, and you cannot tick your way to a way of thinking.

Bad training also skips the managers. Sellers revert the moment the forecast call rewards the old behaviour, and managers run the forecast call. Train the team without its leaders and you have bought a quarter of good intentions.

## What changes when it works

Two things, and you can measure both.

Your forecast gets honest. A forecast is only as reliable as the qualification underneath it. When every deal on the commit line has a quantified Metric, a met Economic Buyer, a tested Champion and a mapped Paper Process, the commit line stops carrying hope. The forecast call turns into an inspection. Do we belong in this deal? Where is it strong? Where is it weak, and why? What moves it forward? Four questions, every deal, every week.

Your team qualifies out. Deals that were never going to close get found early, and the time goes into the deals that can. Nobody regrets qualifying out. The rep gets their quarter back, the customer who was never going to buy gets left alone, and the forecast stops lying.

## My position

Go all in or do not start. MEDDICC is binary. You either use it or you do not, and a team that runs it on the big deals and skips it on the small ones has not adopted it. Adoption takes a full sales cycle, because you need deals qualified under the new rules from first meeting to close before the numbers move. Budget for the cycle and inspect every week.

Pick a provider on three questions. Who wrote the content, and have they carried a quota? How are the managers trained? What happens after the sessions end? Thin answers, keep looking.
